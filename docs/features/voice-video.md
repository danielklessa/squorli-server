**Channel list after a server restart (18 September 2026, user's report):** "wenn der Server neu gestartet wird und noch Leute im Sprachkanal sind (livekit wird nicht neu gestartet) werden die Clients in der Kanalliste nicht mehr angezeigt. im Kanal sieht man aber die verbundenen Clients korrekt." The list is the server's `VoicePresence` (kept per WebSocket, in memory), the stage is LiveKit: voice outlives the chat server's process and every reconnect of the chat socket, the presence did not come back. Two cures, because desktop apps up to 0.1.2 carry the old client: (1) the client says its channel again after every reconnect (`ServerConnection.voiceChannelId`, see `docs/features/afk.md`, same day); (2) the server asks LiveKit at every hello (`LivekitAdmin.roomsByIdentity`, one listing shared for 3 s because after a restart everybody returns at once; identity = user id, room = channel id) and puts a member who still sits in a room back into the list, marked `restored`, unless a connection of theirs already has an entry. A restored entry is a guess about which of the user's connections is in voice: it gives way when any of their connections says `voice.join` or `voice.leave` (`dropRestored`), and the heartbeat (30 s) drops it once LiveKit no longer lists the participant (a page reloaded while in voice is listed for a few more seconds). LiveKit unreachable = nothing is restored and nothing dropped. **No sound on that (user, same day: "Im Falle, dass die Clients schon im livekit waren sollen sie auch keinen Beitritts-Sound triggern"):** nothing to change, the cues come from LiveKit's `ParticipantConnected`/`ParticipantDisconnected` and the own join/leave (`voiceClient.ts`), never from `voice.state`; keep it that way. Checked with a test server on :3001 restarted under a headless Chrome sitting in a voice channel: today's client back in the list after the restart (second `voice.join` sent); a client whose voice events were swallowed (= an old one) back in the list through LiveKit; that client hanging up without its `voice.leave` arriving: entry gone after 11 s; cue tones started: 2 at the join, none at either restart, 2 at the hang-up; no console errors. Not checked: several members, two connections of one user, the production server behind its proxy.

**The whole stage in a window of its own (18 September 2026, user's wish: "man soll auch den gesamten Bühnenbereich auspoppen können"):** a button in the stage's head (`stage.popoutStage`, next to "Sprecher | Kacheln") moves the stage into its own window, e.g. onto a second monitor, while the main window keeps showing the text channel; the same button there (`stage.restoreStage`) or closing the window brings it back. Same technique as the video pop-outs: `openPopoutWindow()` (`VideoWindows.tsx`, now shared) opens an empty window with the client's styles and `StageWindow.tsx` (`useStageWindow`, at App level) renders the SAME `<VoiceStage>` into it through a portal, so there is one client and one voice connection. `App.tsx` builds the stage from the VOICE connection's server (`voiceServer`, `voiceApi`), not from the server on screen: the window survives switching channels, servers and the home view. While it is open `showStage` is false, the dock's "show the stage" button focuses the window instead, and leaving the voice channel or closing the main window closes it. What rendering into another document needed:
- *Menus:* `ContextMenu.tsx` lives in the window of its trigger (`anchor.trigger.ownerDocument`): portal target, size, listeners, `ResizeObserver`. Used by the tile's volume menu and the radio menu.
- *Observers:* `useFittedGrid` (VoiceStage) and `useFits` (RadioControl) take the `ResizeObserver` of the element's own window, like `PopoutElementInfo` already did.
- *Windows opened from that window:* a tile's pop-out passes the window of the click (`onPopout(tile, opener)`); a browser lets only the window with the user's gesture open one.
- *Dialogs:* the camera picker and the desktop app's screen picker are shown in the stage's window when it had the focus at the moment they were asked for (`App.tsx` `pickWindow`, `stageWindow.focusedWindow()`); both take a `win` for their key listener. The window going away cancels an open dialog.
- *Push-to-talk and AFK input:* `usePushToTalk(win, ...)` (taken out of `VoiceDock.tsx`) listens in the main window and in the stage's window; `isTypingTarget` is duck-typed because an element of another window is no `HTMLElement` of this one (tested). `watchActivity` counts input there.
- *Web radio player (Twitch/YouTube):* it is ONE iframe of the main window and moving it would reload it, so it stays there (floating or in its own window); the player tile of the detached stage only says so (`stage.playerInMain`) and registers no `EmbedSlot`.
**Checked (headless Chrome 1400x900 against a test server on :3001 with LiveKit, real mouse events over the DevTools protocol):** the window opens with title "<channel> | Squorli", the camera tile plays there (1280x720, running), the main window shows the text channel; resizing the window refits the grid (643 px -> 494 px); mute from the window reaches the dock; a tile's pop-out opens from the stage's window (third window); a screen share starts from a click in the stage's window; "back" closes the window and the main stage returns; leaving the channel closes the window; no console errors in either window. **Desktop shell (Electron, dev start):** the window opens (window-open handler allows `about:blank`), 1264x761, opaque background also with a window material (the pop-out has no `data-material`), the screen picker comes up IN the stage's window and the share starts from there; back works. **Not checked:** Firefox, push-to-talk in the stage's window by hand, the camera picker in the stage's window with several cameras, a second participant.

**A share's audio plays only for who watches that share (18 September 2026, user's requirement after trying window audio in the desktop app: "er sollte aber am besten nur hörbar sein wenn man das Video selektiert oder auspoppt, es soll nicht jeder im Kanal gezwungen sein den Ton mit zu hören"):** until now everybody in the channel heard a share's audio as soon as it was published. Now `VoiceClient.setScreenAudioListening(tileId, reason, on)` decides: the audio element of a `ScreenShareAudio` track is muted unless somebody listens, and the publication is disabled at LiveKit meanwhile (`setEnabled(false)`, no bandwidth). Who listens: the stage while the user has clicked that share large (`pinned`, not a share that merely moved into focus by itself; leaving the stage ends it) and a pop-out window of that share (`VideoWindows.tsx`). Deafen still wins. The tile's label shows a crossed speaker with "zum Anhören anklicken oder auspoppen" while silent. Applies to browser and app. Tests: `videoAudio.test.ts` (the rule with two listeners and deafen; the pop-out test now listens like the window does). **Fullscreen counts as selected too (user's decision, 18 September 2026):** a share in fullscreen plays its audio, also when it was a small tile (`Tile` in `VoiceStage.tsx` listens to `fullscreenchange`, reason "fullscreen"). Not checked with a real second participant.

**Screen share codec: AV1 to be tested later, VP8 stays for now (18 September 2026, user's decision: "Vorerst für die Web App nicht umstellen"):** the user asked what speaks against AV1 with VP9 as the backup, assuming modern browsers. Nothing was changed; camera and screen share stay on VP8 (`videoCodec: "vp8"` in the room's `publishDefaults`). **To do at a later time:** try AV1 for the screen share only (`videoCodec` in the publish options of `setScreenShareEnabled`, camera stays VP8 with simulcast) and compare with VP8 in the debug view (`?debug`: resolution, fps, kbit/s, `limitation`), once with text and scrolling, once with video or a game, on a weak and a strong sender; keep it (possibly as an option) only if 1080p holds at a reasonable CPU load. Known from reading `livekit-client` 2.22.3, not measured: (1) the backup codec can only be VP8 or H.264 (`backupVideoCodecs`), so "AV1 with VP9 backup" does not exist; the realistic pair is AV1 (or VP9) with a VP8 backup. (2) A backup is needed: Safari decodes AV1 only with a hardware decoder (newer Apple devices). (3) For SVC codecs (AV1, VP9) the SDK publishes a screen share with `scalabilityMode: "L1T3"` and overwrites our `contentHint: "detail"` with `"motion"` (its workaround for Chrome's 5 fps limit and encoder bugs on the screen share path), so under tight bandwidth the resolution may drop instead of the frame rate, which is what we avoided for readable text. (4) AV1 is encoded in software almost everywhere; as soon as one viewer cannot decode it the sender encodes and uploads VP8 in addition. In favour: clearly sharper text at the same bitrate, or roughly 30-50 % less bitrate, which helps senders with a weak upload. The installed Chrome 153 reports AV1 and VP9 for sending and receiving. Tracked as open decision 7 in `docs/PLAN.md`.

**The own tab can be shared (18 September 2026, user's wish: "das eigene Fenster soll auch übertragbar sein"):** the screen picker hid the Squorli tab (`selfBrowserSurface: "exclude"`); it is `"include"` now, in `voiceClient.setScreenShareEnabled()` and on the test page `/test/screenshare.html`. Consequence handled in the same step: the share always asks for audio, and the own tab's audio is everybody else's voice (and the radio), which would come back to them as screen audio. The audio constraint `restrictOwnAudio: true` removes the capturing tab's playback from the captured audio; it also covers "entire screen" with system audio, where the same echo existed before. Chromium 141+ knows it, other browsers ignore it (they deliver no display audio anyway); LiveKit passes the `audio` object to `getDisplayMedia` unchanged (`AudioCaptureOptions` lacks the key, hence the cast). Known and accepted: sharing the tab that shows its own share gives the picture-in-picture tunnel. Checked: the installed Chrome (153, headless) reports `restrictOwnAudio` in `getSupportedConstraints()`; typecheck, tests, build. Not checked: a real share of the own tab with a second listener (needs the picker and two people).

**"Camera: Starting videoinput failed" on Firefox (18 September 2026, user's report, same Firefox user as the pop-out freeze):** that is Firefox's `AbortError` from `getUserMedia` when the device cannot be started. Firefox releases a stopped camera asynchronously and, unlike Chrome, cannot share it with another process; the client ran into that twice: the camera picker's preview track was stopped only in the effect cleanup when React unmounted the dialog, after `onPick` had already asked LiveKit for the same camera (the comment claimed the reverse; `setTrack(null)` released nothing); and off -> on is a mute (stops the track) followed by `restartTrack()` (opens it again). Now `CameraPicker.pick()` releases the preview synchronously (`releasePreview()`) before `onPick`, and `voice/cameraRetry.ts` (`retryCameraBusy`, tested) retries one opening after 600 ms when the error is `AbortError`/`NotReadableError`, used by `setCameraEnabled(true)` and the picker preview. A camera held by another program fails the retry too; `setCameraEnabled` then shows `voice.errCameraBusy` (close other programs with camera access) instead of the bare message. `AbortError` no longer counts as a user cancel for the camera (there is no browser picker to cancel; that reading swallowed device failures silently); `NotAllowedError` still does. Not checked: a real Firefox with a camera (none on the dev machine; the mechanism is documented Firefox behaviour, the sequencing bug is visible in the code).

**Camera froze in a pop-out on Firefox (18 September 2026, user's report: "wenn auf Firefox die Cam-Übertragung ausgepoppt wird, friert die Kamera in dem neuen Fenster ein"):** LiveKit's adaptiveStream watches every attached `<video>` with ONE `IntersectionObserver` created in the main window (`root: null`, `HTMLElementInfo`). Firefox never reports an element of another window as intersecting (Gecko's implicit root is the observer's own top-level document; Chrome measures against the element's document), so LiveKit took the pop-out's view for invisible, paused the track 100 ms after attaching, and the window kept the first frames. Fix: `PopoutElementInfo` in `videoDisplay.ts` implements LiveKit's exported `ElementInfo` for a view in another window (`visible` always true, the window shows nothing else; size from the element; a `ResizeObserver` of the pop-out's own window so the simulcast layer follows the window's size) and `attachVideoView()` registers it through `track.observeElementInfo()` before `attach()` (LiveKit then skips its own info for the element; `detach()` stops it). Remote tracks only; local tracks have no adaptiveStream. Verified with a Firefox nightly zip (158, unpacked into the scratchpad, headless, popups allowed through `user.js`) and headless Chrome, first with a plain probe (Firefox: main-window observer `isIntersecting: false`, the pop-out's own observer `true`; a main-realm `MediaStream` plays fine in the pop-out), then with a real `RemoteVideoTrack` (canvas stream, `adaptiveStream` on) bundled with esbuild: `track.attach()` -> visibility `true, false` in Firefox (paused), `attachVideoView()` -> stays `true`; Chrome `true` both ways. Not checked: a real LiveKit session in Firefox, release Firefox (the mechanism is old Gecko behaviour, not a nightly detail).

**Tile stayed black after somebody turned their camera off (18 September 2026, user's report: "wenn ein Benutzer seine Cam-Übertragung beendet, passiert es häufig, dass die Kachel schwarz bleibt"):** LiveKit's `setCameraEnabled(false)` does not unpublish the camera, it MUTES the track (it stays published; only screen shares are unpublished). Receivers get `TrackMuted`, and the client answered that only with `refreshParticipants()`, never `refreshTiles()`, although `refreshTiles()` already skipped muted cameras (`!cam.isMuted`). So the tile stayed, showing black, until any other event rebuilt the tiles (a stream-state change, someone joining), which is why it happened "often" and not always. Now `TrackMuted`/`TrackUnmuted` also call `refreshTiles()`; turning the camera back on shows the tile again for the same reason. Own tile was fine (`setCameraEnabled` refreshes explicitly). Checked: typecheck, tests, build; not checked in a real session.

**Joining failed with "Constraints could not be satisfied" and a proxy hint (18 September 2026, user's report from community.k9-kommando.de):** the message is the browser's `OverconstrainedError` from `getUserMedia`, not a connection error: the microphone stored under Einstellungen > Audiogeräte is requested with `deviceId: { exact }`, and the device was gone (unplugged, or the browser handed out new device ids). The join caught every error in one place and wrapped it in `voice.connErrGeneric` ("check /rtc/validate ... the proxy does not forward /rtc"), which sent the operator looking at a proxy that was fine (`/rtc/validate` returned 401 when checked). Now: `openMic()` in `micPipeline.ts` retries without the device when the chosen one is gone (`OverconstrainedError`/`NotFoundError`, recognised by `name` because Chromium's is no `Error` instance), so the default microphone takes over; the stored choice is kept, so the device is used again once it is back, and the settings show the device actually in use (`inputDeviceId` = `activeDeviceId()`); the debug log notes the fallback. This covers the join, the switch in the settings and the republish after a profile change. A microphone that still fails (no permission, no device at all) is reported as `voice.errMic` without the proxy hint; `setInputDevice()` reports it the same way instead of an unhandled rejection; `errorText()` falls back to the error's name when it has no message. The join still ends when the microphone fails (listening without a microphone was not asked for). Checked: `micPipeline.test.ts` (4, fake `getUserMedia`), typecheck, tests, build. Not checked: a real browser with an unplugged device.

**Screen share froze in the pop-out or in fullscreen (17 September 2026, user's report: "manchmal bleibt ein Screenshare hängen, wenn er ausgepoppt oder im Vollscreen ist"):** LiveKit's adaptiveStream pauses EVERY received video five seconds after the MAIN page is hidden (`pauseVideoInBackground`, default on; it only knows `document.visibilityState` of the page that loaded it, a video element in a pop-out window does not count, only Picture-in-Picture does). Hidden is also a main window that is minimized or, on Windows, completely covered by other windows, which is exactly how a pop-out on a second monitor gets used. Now `adaptiveStream: { pauseVideoInBackground: false }` (`voiceClient.ts`) and **each video view decides for its own window**: `TrackVideo` (`VideoWindows.tsx`) detaches its element once its `ownerDocument` has been hidden for 5 s and attaches it again when visible (`videoDisplay.ts` `watchDocumentHidden`, tested); a track without an attached element is paused by adaptiveStream as before, so a hidden tab without pop-outs still receives no video. Verified in headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol with a real `RemoteVideoTrack` (canvas stream) shown in the page and in a pop-out, main window minimized: before, both tracks turned invisible after about 6 s; now only the page's view does and comes back with the window, the pop-out's stays visible throughout. Not checked: a real LiveKit session, Windows' occlusion of a covered (not minimized) window.

**Playback volume per person, 0-200 % (17 September 2026, user's wish):** right-click on a member in a voice channel (sidebar), on their tile in the stage or the member list's menu shows "Lautstärke für mich" with a slider, the percentage and a reset to 100 % (`UserVolumeControl.tsx`); not for yourself. Local playback only, nothing is sent. Stored per device in `chat.userVolumes.v1`, keyed by the person's **public key** so it holds on every server (`voice/userVolumes.ts`, tested; only real adjustments are stored); deliberately not in the directory account, because it depends on the headset in front of this browser and is a map without bound. `VoiceClient.getUserVolume/setUserVolume(publicKey)` also work without a connection; `setPeerKeys` / `JoinOptions.peerKeys` map LiveKit identities to keys (`App.tsx`), bots fall back to `id:<identity>`. Up to 100 % the `<audio>` element's volume does it; above, a `GainNode` on the speaker meter's `MediaStreamAudioSourceNode` plays into the shared `AudioContext` while the element stays attached at volume 0 (Chromium feeds a remote stream into Web Audio only while an element plays it). Consequences: deafening sets that gain to 0 (`setDeafened` -> `applyUserVolumes`), a context that is not running falls back to 100 % instead of silence (re-applied on `statechange`), and the boosted voice follows the voice output device only where `AudioContext.setSinkId` exists (Chromium; elsewhere the system default). The volume control of a camera pop-out is the same per-person value (slider to 200 % there, screen audio stays 0-100 %). `ContextMenu` arrow navigation includes `[data-menu-item]` (the slider). Typecheck, all tests and build green; not checked in a browser with real audio.

**Permission to watch camera and screen (17 September 2026, user's requirement):** new role permission `VIEW_VIDEO` ("Kamera- und Bildschirmübertragungen sehen" / "Watch camera and screen shares", Admin > Roles). A member whose roles do not carry it hears the voice channel but receives no camera, no screen share and no screen audio; the stage shows avatars and a notice while somebody is sharing. **Guests do not have it (user's decision):** the default role "Gast" comes without it on fresh servers (`DEFAULT_EVERYONE_PERMISSIONS` unchanged = 1152), "Mitglied" has it (`DEFAULT_MEMBER_PERMISSIONS` = 24000); on existing servers migration 0013 grants it to every non-default role and leaves the default role alone, so pure guests stop seeing shares after the update. Enforced by LiveKit through the senders' track subscription permissions, see `apps/web/src/voice/AGENTS.md` "Watching camera/screen". Typecheck, all tests and the smoke test green; not checked in a browser with real cameras.

**Join and leave cues (16 September 2026):** Four different short sounds mark voice-room changes: you joining, you leaving, someone else joining your room, someone else leaving it. `apps/web/src/voice/sounds.ts` synthesizes them with Web Audio (no audio assets): your own pair is a sine C5/G5, the pair for other people a shorter, quieter triangle E5/A5; rising means arriving, falling means leaving. `voiceClient.ts` triggers them on a completed join, on leaving, and on LiveKit's participant events; switching between rooms plays only the join cue, a failed connection attempt plays nothing, participants already present within 1.5 s of connecting are not announced, and deafening silences all cues. Settings tab "Töne"/"Sounds" in `VoiceDock.tsx` has one switch per cue with a preview button plus a shared volume, stored per device in `chat.voice.v1` (`sounds`). `sounds.test.ts` covers the cue shapes, the stored-settings merge and the play decision.

**Video pop-outs and fullscreen (15 September 2026):** Camera and screen tiles expose separate-window and fullscreen buttons. `VideoWindows.tsx` manages one window per track at App level, retaining it across text/stage view changes, moving video display into the pop-out with a restore placeholder in the main stage, and closing windows when their track disappears or the main page closes. Pop-outs have no app frame or app header/footer; browser chrome (including the address bar) remains browser-controlled. Only the initial pop-out size follows the displayed video dimensions or track settings (16:9 fallback), limited to available screen space. After opening, window size is entirely user-controlled; metadata, format changes, manual resizing and fullscreen transitions never trigger automatic resizing; controls reuse the main-stage styles: close and fullscreen buttons at the top right, with per-feed playback controls at the bottom right. `VideoAudioControls.tsx` shares the volume slider, percentage and local mute toggle between received screen shares in the stage and pop-outs. Stage window/audio controls appear on hover or control keyboard focus (always visible on touch); pop-out controls follow the hover/focus rules below. Muting sets only the selected remote track volume to zero and restores its previous level on unmute, including across pop-out/restore; global deafen still takes precedence. Sender labels remain at the bottom left and audio controls at the bottom right on the same row. The control width is capped at 60% of the tile and names truncate within the remaining space to prevent overlap. Percentage outputs use intrinsic width with a four-character minimum so 100% fits. Controls hide when the window loses focus or the pointer leaves (keyboard-focused controls remain accessible; touch controls stay visible while the window is focused). A bottom-left label shows the sender using the voice server member name, including live renames; double-click or F/Enter toggles fullscreen. Existing audio elements move between documents: microphone audio follows camera pop-outs, screen audio follows screen pop-outs. This preserves one playback instance, volume, output-device selection and global deafen. Closing/restoring a pop-out returns its video and audio to the main window. Autoplay failures show a temporary unblock button. Fullscreen uses the target element's document, with translated blocked/unsupported/failure messages. Shared view detach and fullscreen behavior have unit coverage in `videoDisplay.test.ts`. Real multi-window/media browser acceptance is pending; no browser is connected in the editing environment.

**Camera right after a role change: "failed to publish track, insufficient permissions" (18 September 2026, user's report):** the publish grants of a LiveKit token are fixed when a member joins the voice channel, and the server only adjusted them at runtime for the stream block and the AFK channel. A member who got "Mitglied" (or owner status, or whose role gained `STREAM_VIDEO`) while sitting in a channel saw the camera and screen buttons at once, because the client gets its new permissions with the structure broadcast, but LiveKit still only allowed the microphone until they left and joined again. The other direction was open too: a camera stayed on after the permission was taken away. Now `livekit/sync.ts` (`syncStreamGrantsOf`) brings LiveKit in line after `PUT /api/members/:id/roles`, `PUT /api/members/:id/owner`, `PATCH /api/roles/:id` (when the permissions changed) and `DELETE /api/roles/:id`: grants via `setCanStream`, and when the permission is gone also `stopStreams`; members in the AFK channel stay silenced. No client change, no protocol change. **Microphone boost (18 September 2026; user's report: "der Mikrofonpegel ist bei vielen Leuten extrem niedrig, woran kann das liegen und können wir da Abhilfe schaffen?"):** the client had no gain of its own; it relied on the browser's automatic gain control (`autoGainControl`, off in stereo channels) and the voice activation opens at RMS 0.04. Built: `voice/micBoost.ts` (pure, tested) and its place in `micPipeline.ts`: source -> gain -> clipping guard (WaveShaper with a soft knee at 0.8, identity below it; deliberately no DynamicsCompressorNode, which adds make-up gain to every signal) -> level meter, gate and published track, so the threshold means what the others hear. `AutoGain` learns from the raw input while someone speaks (speech is told from a noise floor, independent of the gate, which sits behind the boost), only ever boosts (1..6, target RMS 0.1), comes down faster than it goes up and remembers its gain per microphone (`chat.micBoost.v1`). Einstellungen > Sprechen: "Leises Mikrofon automatisch anheben" (default on) or a slider 100-600 %, the gain applied right now is shown there and in the debug view ("Pegel / Tor · x..."). Per device and NOT in the directory account (decision by Claude: how quiet a microphone is belongs to the computer, like the device selection). Stereo channels stay unboosted.
**What the measurement says (same day, real client in headless Chrome with a WAV file as the microphone, debug view read for 12 s):** Chromium's own control brings speech of RMS 0.003, 0.012 and 0.1 in the file all to about 0.085 behind the pipeline, gate open ~90 % of the time, and the automatic ends at x1.0 in all three; by hand x3 gives 0.24 with peaks of 0.44 (chain and guard work). So in Chromium (and the desktop app) a quiet capture is NOT the cause of the report, and the automatic changes nothing there. Open causes, not checked: Firefox/Safari (weaker control; could not be measured, their fake microphone has a fixed level), real microphones with noise (the control lifts noise less willingly than a clean file), stereo channels (no control at all, raw level), and perception: about 0.085 RMS (-21 dBFS) is the usual WebRTC level and quieter than Discord, music or games, and the level bar (level x 400) sits at about a third while speaking normally, which looks low. The user was asked what exactly people see or hear; raising everyone's level (a higher target) or a master volume for voices are the candidates if it is perception.

**Cause found: stereo channel (18 September 2026, user's answer: "Pegelanzeige ist niedrig und Schwellwert muss nach ganz links gedreht werden. Die anderen hören sie auch sehr leise. Sowohl in der App als auch im Browser (Firefox), der Kanal ist Stereo"):** a stereo channel captures without the browser's processing (it would mix down to mono), so also without its gain control, and the microphone arrived at its raw level. The entry above still excluded stereo from the boost; that is reversed: **the boost runs in every channel** (gain and clipping guard keep both channels), the largest gain is now x12 (+21.6 dB; a raw microphone set low in the system needs it, slider 100-1200 %), and the learned gain is remembered per microphone AND capture mode (`<device>|stereo`), because the raw level of the same microphone differs a lot between the two. Measured with the same setup as above, channel switched to stereo by REST: boost off, file RMS 0.012 -> level 0.017 and the gate open 0 % of the time (the report, reproduced); automatic -> gain 6, level 0.066, gate open 88 %; file RMS 0.003 -> gain 11.9 (the maximum), level 0.030, gate open 85 %; the second join starts at the remembered gain (5.9 instead of 1). The first join with a quiet microphone needs about three to five seconds of speech until the gate opens; after that the remembered gain applies from the first word. Advice for operators: a channel people TALK in belongs on mono (the default): only there the browser's echo cancellation, noise suppression and gain control work and silence costs no bandwidth (DTX); stereo is for music.
